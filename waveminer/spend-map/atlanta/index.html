What's different here

Four ways this market differs

  • The build money is landing here now.

    Atlanta overtook Northern Virginia for total construction in H1 2026, with 2,882 MW underway (up 52.3%) and pricing for 10 MW+ deals up 14.5%.1 Build-phase rows that are thin in Tampa are live here.

  • Labor is the binding constraint.

    Georgia became the busiest state for data-center construction by June 2026. One electrical contractor, Ace Electric, netted more than 500 employees this year, hiring about 700 to do it at 30% turnover. Roughly a third of Georgia firms surveyed report more labor competition from data centers.5 Even experienced electricians need retraining for data-center work.

  • The installed base is young and doubling.

    Installed capacity roughly doubled to about 1.8 GW in a year (vendor estimate).2 A young base means little replacement or refresh work today and a lot of it later. Service businesses that start now grow with it.

  • Pushback is spreading county by county.

    Atlanta banned data centers in the BeltLine overlay in 2024. At least eight Georgia jurisdictions adopted moratoria after March 2025, and county ordinances set noise caps (Bartow: 65 dB by day, 55 dB at night), water rules and buffers.6 The state sales-tax exemption survived a 2026 repeal attempt; a state audit put 2025 forgone revenue at about $474M.7

Local rules

What creates or blocks work here

  • Contractor licenseGeorgia requires a state electrical contractor license; commercial data-center work needs the unrestricted class, and qualifying agents pass a state exam.9
  • PowerThe PSC allowed minimum bills and longer contracts for new large-load customers in January 2025,4 and approved 10 GW of new Georgia Power resources in December 2025, about 90% driven by expected data-center demand from 2028 to 2031.3
  • TaxSales-tax exemption survived a 2026 repeal attempt.7
  • Local ordinancesBeltLine overlay ban (2024); moratoria in at least eight jurisdictions since March 2025; noise, water and buffer rules in counties such as Bartow, Forsyth and Jones.6
For people who live and work in Atlanta

Six questions only a local can answer

Answer as many as you like, by text or email to Patrick or through patrick@workbypk.com. Short answers are fine. "I don't know" is useful too. Anything you share is kept anonymous.

  1. Bottlenecks. On the data-center jobs you see, which trades are the bottleneck right now, and what is a journeyman electrician getting paid?
  2. Handover work. Who does post-construction cleaning, commissioning and load-bank testing at handover: local firms or national crews that travel in?
  3. After the build. When a campus is finished, who picks up maintenance: the builder, the OEM dealers, or the owner's facilities firm?
  4. Noise limits. Are county noise limits changing when or how generators get tested?
  5. Other backup-power customers. Outside data centers, who else runs large generator fleets nearby: hospitals, logistics hubs, manufacturers?
  6. Owners. If you wanted to buy or partner with a small electrical or generator service firm here, who would you call first?
