Follow $1 billion

The four largest hyperscalers are guiding to roughly $710–733B of 2026 capex, with $301B already spent in the first half; Goldman forecasts $1.01T for 2027.4,5 Epoch AI's cost model of a typical 1 GW AI campus splits the money like this.1

What the numbers say

Five readings

  • 69¢ of each dollar is out of reach.

    Servers and networking take $689M of every $1B. A small operator competes for the $302M facility slice, and half of that is electrical.

  • The recurring pool is small per site but it compounds.

    About $3.2M a year of maintenance per $1B of capex, growing with every megawatt energized and still flowing if new capex stalls. JLL counts 66 GW under construction in North America.3 At Epoch's $120M per GW, that is roughly $8B a year of maintenance once it is running (rough arithmetic on a stylized model).

  • Mundane does not mean undiscovered.

    Comfort Systems' management said service work on the growing installed base of data centers could become a longer-term revenue stream.7 CBRE paid about $1.2B for Pearce to do UPS and generator service in-house.10 PE closed about 125 fire and life-safety deals in 2025.29 The overlooked part is the small end: hundreds of regional service firms with $3–25M of revenue.21

  • The build money mostly lands elsewhere.

    77% of North American capacity under construction is in frontier markets like West Texas, Ohio and Louisiana.3 A Florida tracker shows 5 of the 8 projects it follows have been stopped.26 A Tampa Bay business needs a second demand base, and Florida has one: 3,771 nursing homes and ALFs under a 96-hour emergency-power mandate.23,24

  • The shortage has an end date.

    Switchgear and transformer lead times run roughly one to five years today, with relief expected unevenly after 2028.11,12 Supply plays should be underwritten to close when factories catch up.

Generator testing, load bank & fuel serviceField service · installed base
$1.8–2.4B/yrUS C&I generator service (vendor est.)
Cycle: lowFragmentedFL: yes
Top candidate. Code-mandated, fragmented, with a Florida base that does not depend on data centers.
Why it recurs
NFPA 110 requires a monthly exercise, an annual supplemental load test when monthly runs don't reach 30% load, and a full-load test every 36 months for Level 1 systems.19 Data-center practice is annual at 100% load, semiannual for Tier III/IV.20
Density
A 500 MW–1 GW AI campus runs 20 to 100+ generators.21 Fort Meade's developer plans 100% backup generation for 1.2 GW.25
Scarce input
OEM-certified technicians (12–24 months to replace one) and a master electrician license, often held by a single person.22
Who's there
OEM dealer networks take warranty and national contracts. Independents do most day-to-day work, mostly within one metro; hundreds of regional firms run $3–25M of revenue.21 CBRE bought Pearce to self-perform this work.10
Price to buy
5–7× EBITDA for $3–10M dealers; 6.5–9× for $5–20M multi-brand firms with 40–65% recurring revenue. Hurricane years can inflate EBITDA 40–80%, so normalize over five years.22
Florida
691 nursing homes and 3,080 ALFs must keep emergency power and fuel for 96 hours under state law.23,24 Florida adds a state electrical license and county standby permits.22

What this changed in the method

Seven fields every opportunity card now carries

Running one real theme end to end showed what a technology-focused template misses when the opportunity is a contractor, a supplier or a service business.

  • phaseBuild-phase, installed base or refresh. Decides cycle exposure.
  • recurrence_driverCode mandate, wear-out interval or technology shift, with the citation.
  • scarce_inputWhat is short, who holds it, and whether a new entrant adds supply or just competes for it.
  • crowdingNamed consolidators, deal counts and multiples paid.
  • hedge_marketDemand that survives if this wave's capex halves.
  • geographyWhere the money physically lands versus where the founder can operate.
  • window_closeFor shortage plays, when supply is expected to catch up.

How this was made

WaveMiner is a personal opportunity-intelligence project: trace a technology shift to the bottleneck it creates, the buyer who pays to remove it, and the cheapest real-world test. This note is its first full test on a real theme.

Patrick's input

  • Developed the WaveMiner concept and specification: evidence, then bottleneck, workflow, buyer, experiment and a build-or-kill decision, instead of a news digest or an idea generator.
  • Set the evidence rules: sources graded A–D, every claim tied to a source, explicit kill criteria, and “no material change” counted as a valid result.
  • Widened the scope from technology companies to any business whose demand rises because of the wave: contractors, suppliers, field service, staffing, distribution and acquisitions. The north star is to own something whose demand grows as AI and robotics keep expanding.
  • Pointed the search at the mundane corners that most people are not looking at.
  • Chose to run one real theme end to end, the AI data-center buildout, before automating anything.
  • Treats direct conversations with operators, contractors and advisors as the real test, so customer evidence outranks research.

Claude's role

  • Stress-tested the specification and suggested additions: capex-cycle exposure, the scarce-input test, geography and hedge markets.
  • Gathered and read the sources, graded each one, ran the arithmetic and built this page.
  • Flagged vendor-sourced and unverified claims so they get checked before anything is acted on.

Claude is Anthropic's AI model. All research was directed and reviewed by Patrick.

Next step: conversations with operators and contractors to test the leading candidates.
