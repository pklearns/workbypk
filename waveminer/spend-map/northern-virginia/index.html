What's different here

Four ways this market differs

  • The installed base is the story.

    Northern Virginia led every primary market in H1 2026 absorption (467.6 MW) at 0.2% vacancy, while construction growth slowed to 16.5% as land and permitting tightened.1 Every installed-base row on the national map is at its largest here.

  • Generators are a regulated fleet.

    Air permits cover more than 13,500 generators across 195 applications. Typical permits cap each unit at 500 hours a year including testing, and require usage logs, reports to DEQ every six months and five years of records on site.3 New permit applications since 1 July 2026 presume Tier 4 controls: selective catalytic reduction, particulate filters and continuous emissions monitoring.4 That adds emissions-equipment upkeep and compliance paperwork on top of ordinary generator service.

  • The build frontier is moving out.

    Loudoun ended by-right data centers in March 2025 and in September 2026 is weighing a pause and an end to the grandfather clause.5 In Prince William, courts voided the Digital Gateway rezoning, supervisors rejected the 1,940-acre Dulles South proposal 8–0, and the county is moving to shrink its data-center overlay district.6 Developers have been looking at Fauquier, Culpeper, Stafford and Spotsylvania since at least 2023.7

  • Operating costs just went up.

    A new electricity consumption tax of $0.011 per kWh on data centers runs from 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2028, capped at $600M a year; the sales-tax exemption survives through 2035.8 From 1 January 2027, data centers drawing 25 MW or more on Dominion's grid must post $1.5M per MW of collateral.9 Higher power costs make efficiency and cooling-optimization work easier to sell.

Local rules

What creates or blocks work here

  • Contractor licenseVirginia requires a Class A contractor license for contracts of $150,000+ or $1M+ a year, with the electrical (ELE) specialty; the firm's qualified individual may also need a DPOR tradesman certification.11
  • Generator air permitsVirginia DEQ permits cap run hours (typically 500 a year including testing) and require logs and six-month reports.3 Tier 4 controls are presumed for new applications from 1 July 2026.4
  • PowerDominion's GS-5 rate class for loads of 25 MW+ takes effect 1 January 2027 with $1.5M per MW of collateral.9
  • TaxSales-tax exemption kept through 2035; electricity consumption tax of $0.011/kWh from July 2026 to June 2028.8
  • ZoningLoudoun: by-right ended March 2025, pause and grandfather-clause removal under consideration.5 Prince William: moving to shrink the overlay district; Digital Gateway voided; Dulles South rejected.6
For people who live and work in Northern Virginia

Six questions only a local can answer

Answer as many as you like, by text or email to Patrick or through patrick@workbypk.com. Short answers are fine. "I don't know" is useful too. Anything you share is kept anonymous.

  1. Generator service. Who services data-center generators near you: OEM dealers, independents, or the operators' own staff? Is anyone known for doing it badly?
  2. Emissions work. Since the Tier 4 rule, who installs and maintains the catalytic reduction and emissions monitors, and who writes the DEQ reports?
  3. Routine vendors. When a facility needs UPS batteries swapped or a hall cleaned, who gets the call? Is it bid out, or always the same vendor?
  4. Outer counties. Are Culpeper, Fauquier, Stafford and Spotsylvania served by Loudoun-area firms driving out, or by local ones?
  5. Other backup-power customers. Outside data centers, who else runs large generator fleets nearby: federal sites, hospitals, others?
  6. Owners. If you wanted to buy or partner with a small generator, electrical or battery service firm here, who would you call first?
