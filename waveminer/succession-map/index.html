Top of funnel

Follow six million handoffs

McKinsey expects roughly 6 million small and mid-size businesses to face an ownership transition by 2035, worth up to $5T, but only about 1 million of them viable to sell.12 Owners 55 and older already hold about half of all employer firms.13 Very little of that reaches an open market, and what does now runs through tighter SBA rules.

What the numbers say

Six readings

  • The biggest market is the businesses that don't sell.

    Roughly five of every six transitions end in closure or a private handoff.12 Their customer lists, service contracts, phone numbers, equipment and trained staff still have value to a competitor, and today mostly go to auctioneers or vanish.

  • Buyers are not the bottleneck.

    SBA 7(a) approvals rose from 47,678 loans in FY2022 to 77,600 in FY2025, search-fund launches hit historic highs, and 44% of buyers call themselves corporate refugees.11 The original thesis loses its "not enough buyers" half.

  • The market got pickier, not smaller.

    Closed deals fell 10% in Q2 2026, yet the average cash-flow multiple rose to 2.7x. The median deal: $692K revenue, $156K cash flow, $349K price.1 Buyers pay for proof, and 78% of them expect to use SBA money.3

  • The rules change on 1 October.

    A seller can no longer paper over weak books with an easy seller note, and rollover structures that kept the seller in are constrained.4,8 Lenders can underwrite from CPA-prepared or reviewed statements when tax returns are messy.10 Clean, lender-grade financials become the scarce input.

  • The middle of your band is thin.

    Tampa's sell-side quality-of-earnings work sits with Kaufman Rossin, CBIZ and CLA at the lower-middle market.16 Main Street belongs to brokers who now carry exit-planning credentials.17 Firms with $150K–$2M of cash flow fall between them.

  • The roll-ups already arrived next door.

    General Catalyst set aside $1.5B to buy accounting and other service firms; Crete reached 30+ accounting firms; Thrive Holdings is betting $1B with OpenAI in the cap table.20,21 The partner firm fits the target profile, which is both a threat and an exit option.

Take to validation

Mundane plays to test first

Ranked on recurrence, crowding and whether they get paid without a deal closing. Each has the cheapest real-world test and a kill line attached.

TOP · Handoff plumbing · acquire

Closure asset harvest

Thesis
When an owner closes instead of selling, the partner firm's clients buy the customer list, service contracts, phone number, equipment and key staff for cash, below any SBA process.
Your seat
Spot the closure early, value the pieces, structure the purchase.
Red team
Customers may not follow; auctioneers and competitors already pick over closures.
Cheapest test
Ask the partner firm how many clients or contacts closed in the last three years and what happened to their customers. Ask five healthy clients what a competitor's customer list would be worth to them.
Kill
Fewer than three closures a year within reach, or clients won't pay for customer lists.
TEST · Handoff plumbing · service

Clean-handoff kit + first 90 days

Thesis
A fixed-fee closing kit (tax clearance, liens, licenses, consents, payroll and plan handoff) that rolls into a post-close CFO retainer for the new owner.
Your seat
Design the package and sell it to brokers and SBA lenders as a referral product.
Red team
Deal attorneys may see it as their turf; small deals may refuse any added fee.
Cheapest test
Show a one-page checklist to two Tampa brokers and two SBA lenders; ask which items fail most and whether they'd refer it.
Kill
No broker or lender would refer it, or attorneys already cover it on small deals.
TEST · Handoff plumbing · Florida-specific

Contractor license continuity

Thesis
Trade firms whose retiring owner is the only license holder have 60 days to replace them. Plan that succession for prep clients, and buy good crews that are about to lose their license.
Your seat
Planning and deal side; a licensed partner supplies the qualifier.
Red team
Renting out a license is a regulatory hazard; the partner must genuinely supervise the work.
Cheapest test
From DBPR records, count four-county contracting firms with one qualifier licensed 25+ years ago. Ask two licensing attorneys how often clients hit the 60-day wall.
Kill
Few single-qualifier firms with long-tenured licenses, or attorneys say the gap is routinely solved in-house.
Also on the table

Advisory-layer plays

Less mundane and more crowded, but timed to the 1 October SBA rule change.

Advisory · Fix a weakness · Stage 3, current

SBA-ready sale prep for owners 55+

Thesis
A fixed-fee package for owners one to five years from exit: lender-grade recast and addbacks, QoE-lite, owner-dependency fixes, and an SBA-compliant key-employee retention plan.
Your seat
Deal structure and retention-plan design. The partner firm owns the books.
Red team
Brokers and exit planners give readiness advice free to win listings. Answer: they sell listings; this sells financeability.
Cheapest test
Score 10 partner-firm clients with owners 55+ against a buyer-ready checklist. Offer three a paid review at $5–10K. Ask two Tampa SBA lenders whether they'd rely on the work.
Kill
Fewer than 2 of 10 would pay, or lenders say it doesn't change underwriting.
Advisory · Acquire · Stage 3→4, next

Add-on acquisition desk for healthy clients

Thesis
Turn the partner firm's strongest clients into buyers of retiring competitors under the favorable Business Expansion category. Owners who never list sell to someone they know.
Your seat
Structuring, financing and the retention plan; a path to minority stakes.
Red team
Brokers already sell to strategics. Licensing for success fees needs checking first.
Cheapest test
Ask five healthy clients whether they'd buy a competitor in 24 months and at what size. Ask two lenders how they'll treat Expansion files after 1 October.
Kill
Fewer than 2 clients interested, or lenders treat Expansion files no differently.
WATCH · Acquire + modernize · Stage 4, next

Local AI-rewired bookkeeping roll-up

Thesis
The partner firm buys retiring bookkeepers in the four counties near 1x revenue, rewires the work with AI, and feeds more owners into the two plays above.
Your seat
Capital allocation and acquisition structure.
Red team
National platforms with billions target the same firms, and the partner firm itself is in their target profile.
Cheapest test
Count local practices with principals 60+ from DBPR and Sunbiz; ask three practice brokers about deal flow.
Kill
Fewer than about 10 plausible targets, or local prices above 1.5x revenue.
Tier A network evidence

Questions for the partner firm

  1. Intent. Of clients with owners 55+, how many have mentioned selling, and in what timeframe?
  2. Closures. When a client or contact closed instead of selling, what happened to its customers, phone number, equipment and staff?
  3. License risk. Which clients depend on a single license holder who is near retirement?
  4. Deal killers. When a client tried to sell, what killed or cut the price: addbacks, owner dependency, customer concentration, or financing?
  5. Buyers. Which healthy clients have bought, or would buy, a competitor?
  6. Pressure. Has a PE or AI accounting platform approached the firm or practices you know? Did the 2024 storms or insurance costs change any client's plans?
